# Tenda launches an onchain microtasking app that pays the moment a task is accepted

### The mobile app lets anyone post or complete paid tasks from anywhere, with settlement built into task completion rather than run as a separate payout process

**[[CITY]] — [[DATE]]** — Tenda today launched a rewarding onchain microtasking application in which payment settles at the moment a completed task is accepted, removing the payout queues, minimum thresholds and holding periods that separate finished work from money on every established platform in the category.

Tenda is available on [[PLATFORMS: iOS / Android / both]] from [[DATE]] in [[MARKETS]].

The app runs one loop: a task is posted, assigned, completed, accepted and paid. Posting and execution both happen onchain, which means a task can be created anywhere and executed anywhere, and settlement is part of finishing the work rather than a process that begins afterwards.

"Nobody ever quit a microtasking app because the tasks were too hard," said [[FOUNDER NAME]], [[TITLE]] of Tenda. "They quit at the payout screen. The work was never the problem — the gap between finishing and being paid is. So we don't have one. On Tenda, paid is a state of the task, not a promise about next month."

### Not a marketplace

Tenda describes itself as an application rather than a marketplace, and the distinction is load-bearing rather than cosmetic.

A marketplace lists supply and demand, introduces the two parties, takes a fee for the introduction and steps back; the work and the payment then happen elsewhere, on separate systems and separate schedules. Tenda holds all five states of a task on one surface — posting, assignment, submission, acceptance and settlement.

That is the technical reason instant settlement is possible at all. Payment cannot be part of task completion if payment lives on a different system than the task.

"A marketplace's job ends at the handshake," said [[FOUNDER NAME]]. "Ours ends when the money has moved."

### Where the category actually breaks

Microtasking is an established market with an established failure point, and it is not the work.

On the largest incumbent platforms, workers outside a short list of countries cannot receive cash at all and are offered gift cards instead. Payout minimums routinely sit above what a casual earner accumulates in a month. Hold periods of two to four weeks are standard. Whole markets have been switched off with little notice, stranding earners who had already done the work. The category's reputation problem lives entirely in the interval between "done" and "paid."

Tenda's design treats that interval as the product. Because settlement happens onchain at the point of acceptance, there is no payout run to schedule, no threshold to accumulate toward, and no separate withdrawal step to be approved.

The geographic consequence is the one the company expects to matter most to earners. "'Work from anywhere' in this industry has usually meant a laptop on a beach," said [[FOUNDER NAME]]. "For the people actually doing this work, it means something much plainer: the app doesn't check your passport before deciding whether you're allowed to be paid."

### For the people posting work

On the other side of the loop, Tenda is aimed at anyone who needs a high volume of small things done without opening a hiring process: growth and operations teams, AI data teams, researchers, community operators and small businesses. Tasks are created in-app and onchain from anywhere, assigned, and paid on acceptance.
