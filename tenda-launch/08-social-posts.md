### LI-1 · Launch day — the category argument

> Every marketplace built for small paid work in the last twenty years optimised the same step.
>
> Matching. Search, ranking, reputation, recommendations, better feeds. Billions of dollars and two decades of product work, all aimed at helping two people find each other faster.
>
> Meanwhile the last step — the money actually arriving — sat almost untouched. A queue. A threshold. A calendar. A country list.
>
> That's why the category's reputation problem lives at the payout screen and not the task screen. Nobody ever quit one of these apps because the tasks were too hard.
>
> Today we're launching Tenda. It's an onchain microtasking app, and its only real claim is about timing: when your completed task is accepted, you're paid in that moment.
>
> One thing we're strict about — Tenda is an application, not a marketplace. That's not branding. A marketplace introduces two parties and steps back, leaving the work and the payment on separate systems. Tenda holds all five states of a task on one surface: post, assign, do, accept, paid.
>
> That's the actual reason instant settlement is possible rather than advertised. You cannot make payment part of finishing a task if the payment lives somewhere else.
>
> It also costs us something real. If you don't hold funds between the work and the payment, you don't get the float — and in this category the float is a business model, not a rounding error. We gave it up deliberately.
>
> Tenda is on [[PLATFORMS]] today. If you've ever done work on a phone and then waited three weeks to be allowed to touch the money, I'd like to know what you think.
>
> #microtasking #productlaunch

### LI-2 · Day 3 — the founder's reason

> I have been on the wrong end of a payout screen.
>
> Not as research. As a person who had done the work, watched the balance sit there, and read the sentence explaining that my country wasn't eligible for cash — but here are some gift cards.
>
> The thing that stayed with me wasn't the money. It was how normal it was. Nobody was embarrassed. The gap between finishing work and being paid for it is so standard in this industry that it isn't even a complaint — it's just the shape of the thing.
>
> When we started building Tenda, the question wasn't "how do we pay faster." It was "why is there a gap at all."
>
> The honest answer is that the gap exists because the work and the money have always lived on different systems. A marketplace introduces you to someone; a payment processor pays you later; a bank decides when later is; a compliance list decides whether later ever comes.
>
> Put the task and the payment on the same system, and the gap stops being necessary. That's what onchain is doing in our product. Not a philosophy. A timing mechanism.
>
> Tenda is live on [[PLATFORMS]]. Post a task from anywhere, take a task from anywhere, get paid when your work is accepted.
>
> The work was never the problem.

### LI-4 · Day 14 — the contrarian one

> The largest labour experiment of the last few years was not run by a labour company.
>
> Hundreds of millions of people spent months tapping a phone screen for tokens, in games that produced nothing anybody wanted and paid in odds rather than money. Most serious commentary treated it as a joke or a mania.
>
> I think it proved something, and I think the wrong lesson got taken from it.
>
> It proved that an enormous number of people will do small, repetitive, low-value actions on a phone, for a long time, if the loop is tight and the payoff feels immediate. That's not a mania. That's a labour supply, and it showed up voluntarily.
>
> What it never had was work anyone actually needed done, or money anyone could actually spend.
>
> We built Tenda on the assumption that the loop was right and the target was wrong. Same mechanic — small task, phone, immediate payoff. Except the task is something a real person commissioned, and the payoff is money that settles the moment your work is accepted.
>
> I might be wrong about this. But "hundreds of millions of people demonstrated a behaviour and everyone decided it was silly" seems like a strange place to stop looking.

## Platform notes

**Where the earner audience actually is:** TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Telegram and WhatsApp groups, and local Facebook groups — *not* LinkedIn and largely not X. LinkedIn and X here are for posters, press, and investors. Budget accordingly: the LinkedIn and X posts above will produce coverage and credibility; the video assets in `09-video-scripts.md` will produce installs.

**Reply strategy:** the most valuable social work at launch is not posting. It's replying, honestly and specifically, in every thread where someone asks "has anyone actually been paid by this." One specific answer with a real number beats ten posts.

**Never:** buy engagement, run giveaway-for-follow campaigns, or use countdown urgency. This audience has been trained by scams to read all three as warnings.
