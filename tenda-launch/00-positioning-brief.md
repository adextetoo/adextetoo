## 2. Audience profile

### 2A. The Earner — the person completing tasks

**Who:** Phone-first, 18–34. Students, junior freelancers, community moderators, data labellers, app testers, translators, survey respondents. Heavily concentrated in Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, the Philippines, Indonesia, India, Vietnam, Brazil, Argentina — the places where the existing platforms accept the *labour* but restrict the *payout*.

**Job to be done:** "Turn the hours between other things, and the phone already in my hand, into money I can actually reach today."

**Fears, in the order they actually occur:**
1. I'll do the work and not get paid.
2. The payout minimum is $50 and I'll never reach it.
3. My country isn't on the withdrawal list — I'll be offered gift cards I can't use.
4. My account gets frozen after I've earned, with no appeal and no human to email.
5. It's crypto, so someone is going to take my money or ask me to deposit first.
6. I'll lose the funds because I don't understand wallets.

**Their actual language** — this is the vocabulary the copy must use:
> "does it pay for real" · "payout proof" · "how much per task" · "cash out" · "withdrawal minimum" · "is it legit" · "no investment needed?" · "how long before it reflects"

Note what is absent: *decentralised, permissionless, protocol, ecosystem, web3-native.* Never lead with those words. The earner does not want a movement. They want the money to land.

**Alternatives they use today:** Amazon Mechanical Turk, Clickworker, Appen, Remotasks, Prolific, Toloka, Freecash, JumpTask, Swagbucks, Telegram tap-to-earn games, and WhatsApp/Telegram gig groups where a stranger promises to send money after.

### 2B. The Poster — the person creating tasks

**Who:** Indie founders, growth and ops leads, AI data teams, researchers, community and DAO operators, event organisers, small local businesses.

**Job to be done:** "Get 300 small things done this week without opening a hiring req, an invoice, or a contractor onboarding flow."

**Fears:**
1. I'll pay for output I can't use and have no recourse.
2. I can't reach workers in the specific market I need.
3. Cross-border payment paperwork costs more than the work does.
4. Coordinating it will eat more of my time than doing it myself would.

**Their language:** "turnaround," "throughput," "cost per task," "sample size," "batch," "acceptance rate," "spot-check."

**Alternatives:** MTurk requester console, Upwork/Fiverr, agencies, interns, DAO bounty boards, and the universal fallback — posting in a Discord and promising USDC.

## 3. Competitive map

| Player | What it is | Where it leaves an opening |
|---|---|---|
| **Amazon Mechanical Turk** | The incumbent microtask platform | Desktop-era. Worker payouts practically restricted to a handful of countries; elsewhere, gift cards. Mass rejections with no appeal. Requester side needs a US bank. The wait between work and money is structural. |
| **Clickworker / Appen / Remotasks / Toloka** | Real work, real money, at scale | Centralised payout with hold periods and thresholds. Country availability changes without warning — workers have watched entire markets get switched off after they'd earned. |
| **Freecash / JumpTask / Swagbucks** | Consumer "earn" apps; cash out from as little as $0.50, tasks in the ~$2–5 range | The work is offers, installs and surveys — engagement, not commissioned labour. A poster can't get their own job done here. |
| **Layer3 / Galxe / Zealy** | Onchain quests, mobile-friendly, large audiences | The "work" is marketing engagement — follow, retweet, mint — paid in points and airdrop odds. Nothing anyone needed doing actually gets done. |
| **Dework / Gitcoin bounties** | Genuinely onchain work | Developer-shaped, desktop-shaped, bounty-sized. Review cycles run days, not seconds. |
| **Telegram tap-to-earn** | Proved that hundreds of millions of people will perform tiny repeated actions on a phone | Pointed at nothing. Speculative payout, zero output. |

**The pattern:** every competitor's worst reviews are about the money, not the work. Search any of these names with the phrase "does it actually pay" and the category's entire trust problem surfaces in one query. The work step is solved. The settlement step is where the category is rotten.

## 4. The three insights the campaign exploits

**Insight 1 — The wait *is* the reputation.**
Nobody quits a microtask platform because the tasks were hard. They quit at the payout screen. The interval between "done" and "paid" is where every platform in this category hides its worst behaviour: minimums, holds, geo-restrictions, silent bans. So the campaign leads with settlement, not with tasks.

**Insight 2 — "Anywhere" is an access claim, not a lifestyle claim.**
In this category, "work from anywhere" has been sold as a laptop on a beach. For Tenda's earner it means something much less romantic and much more valuable: *the app does not check your passport before deciding whether you're allowed to be paid.* Reframe "remote" from aesthetic to eligibility. This is the line that lands in Lagos, Manila and Buenos Aires and doesn't land in San Francisco — which is fine, because that's where the users are.

**Insight 3 — Tap-to-earn proved the loop; it just aimed it at nothing.**
The behaviour is established at enormous scale: people will do small repeated things on a phone, for money, happily. What the tap-to-earn wave lacked was an output anyone wanted and a payout anyone could spend. Tenda runs the same loop and points it at work someone actually commissioned. This is the strongest *press* hook — journalists like a story about a proven behaviour aimed at the wrong target.

## 5. Positioning

### Core benefit statement
> **Tenda closes the gap between finishing work and getting paid for it.**

One sentence. No feature list. No jargon. Everything else in the campaign is an elaboration of it.

### Positioning formula
> **Tenda** helps **people who post and complete small paid tasks** **turn finished work into settled payment in the same moment**, by **running posting, assignment, completion and payout as one onchain loop inside a single mobile app — rather than as a listing in one place and a payout somewhere else.**

### Campaign angle
> **The gap between done and paid.**

Every asset lives inside this tension. The gap is where the category's fear, folklore and bad reviews live. Tenda's product claim is not "better tasks" or "more tasks" — it is that the gap is gone.

The angle has three practical properties:
- It is **specific** — it names an interval, not a vibe.
- It is **verifiable** — a user finds out whether we're telling the truth within one task.
- It is **not borrowable** — a competitor can't run this campaign without first rebuilding their settlement.

### The category claim: application, not marketplace

This is a hard constraint (F5) and it needs an argument, not just a correction, because every journalist's first instinct will be to write "marketplace."

> A marketplace's job is finished when two parties find each other. It lists, it matches, it takes a cut, and then it stands back while the actual work, the actual chasing and the actual invoice happen somewhere else. Its product is the listing.
>
> Tenda's job is finished when the work is done and the money has moved. Posting, assignment, submission, acceptance and settlement all happen on one surface. Its product is the completed task.
>
> **Paid is a state of the task, not a promise about next month.**

That last line is the campaign's single most portable sentence. It appears in the press release, the pitch emails, the landing page and the founder quote bank — and nowhere is it padded with adjectives.

## 6. Tone profile — locked before any copy

**Voice in one line:** a plain-spoken operator who has been on the wrong end of a payout screen.

**Six rules:**
1. **Concrete over abstract.** Name the interval, the action, the amount. "Paid the moment the task is accepted" beats "instant rewards."
2. **Money in plain words.** *Paid. Money. Cash out.* Never *monetise, unlock value, earn rewards, remuneration.*
3. **The chain is plumbing, not identity.** Onchain is *how* Tenda settles instantly, and that's the only reason it's ever mentioned. Never open a sentence with "web3."
4. **Verbs carry the weight.** If a sentence needs an adjective to be impressive, the sentence is wrong.
5. **Respect the earner.** They are not "users completing microtasks." They are people whose time is already spent by the time we owe them.
6. **Dry, rare humour — from accuracy, never from wordplay.** "No net-30. There is no net anything" is allowed. Puns on "Tenda"/"tender" are not.

**Banned words** — delete on sight, in every asset:
`revolutionary` · `game-changing` · `cutting-edge` · `world-class` · `seamless` · `empower` · `unlock` · `disrupt` · `leverage` · `frictionless` · `ecosystem` (meaning "our product") · `In today's competitive landscape` · `thousands trust us` · `learn more` · `the future of work` *(as an unearned claim; may be questioned, never asserted)*

**Also banned by category:** describing Tenda as a marketplace, a platform for freelancers, a gig marketplace, a jobs board, a talent network, or a quest platform.

**House vocabulary:**

| Concept | Say | Never say |
|---|---|---|
| Person creating work | **Poster** | client, employer, requester, buyer |
| Person completing work | **Earner** | worker, gig worker, taskee, seller |
| Unit of work | **Task** | gig, job listing, bounty, quest |
| The product | **App / application** | marketplace, platform, network |
| Payment event | **Settlement** / "paid" | payout processing, disbursement, withdrawal request |
| The five states | **The loop:** Post → Assign → Do → Accept → Paid | funnel, journey, pipeline |
