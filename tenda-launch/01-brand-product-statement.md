## The loop

Tenda has one mechanic, and every explanation of the product should return to it. Five states, no gap.

```
  POST  ──▶  ASSIGN  ──▶  DO  ──▶  ACCEPT  ──▶  PAID
   │                                              ▲
   └──────────── all of it onchain ───────────────┘
```

| State | What happens | Who acts |
|---|---|---|
| **Post** | A task is created onchain, from anywhere | Poster |
| **Assign** | The task is assigned to an earner | [[VERIFY: assignment model — open claim, application, invite, or algorithmic match?]] |
| **Do** | The work is completed and submitted in-app | Earner |
| **Accept** | The submission is accepted | [[VERIFY: acceptance model — poster review, automated check, or both?]] |
| **Paid** | Settlement happens onchain, instantly | The app |

The sentence that carries this everywhere:
> **On Tenda, "paid" isn't the next step after the task. It's the last state of the task.**

## Why we say *application*, not *marketplace*

This is not brand pedantry. It describes where the product's surface area actually sits, and it is the single most likely thing for a writer to get wrong.

**A marketplace** is a venue. It lists supply, lists demand, introduces the two, takes a cut of the introduction, and then withdraws. The work happens elsewhere. The payment happens elsewhere, later, on someone else's schedule. A marketplace's product is the listing, and its job is finished at the handshake.

**An application** is a place where something is done. On Tenda, the task is created, assigned, worked, submitted, accepted and paid inside one surface. Nothing is handed off to email, to a bank, to an invoice, or to a payout run at the end of the month. Tenda's product is the completed task, and its job is finished when the money has moved.

The difference is not philosophical. It is why instant settlement is possible at all: **you cannot make payment part of task completion if payment happens on a different system than the task.**

> **A marketplace ends at the handshake. Tenda ends at the payment.**

## Voice

**In one line:** a plain-spoken operator who has been on the wrong end of a payout screen.

**Six rules**

1. **Concrete over abstract.** Name the interval, the action, the amount. "Paid the moment the task is accepted" beats "instant rewards."
2. **Money in plain words.** *Paid. Money. Cash out.* Never *monetise, unlock value, remuneration.*
3. **The chain is plumbing, not identity.** Onchain is *how* Tenda settles instantly. That is the only reason it is ever mentioned. Never open with "web3."
4. **Verbs carry the weight.** If a sentence needs an adjective to be impressive, rewrite the sentence.
5. **Respect the earner.** By the time Tenda owes someone money, their time is already spent. Write like that matters.
6. **Dry, rare humour — from accuracy, never wordplay.** *"No net-30. There is no net anything."* is in voice. Any pun on "Tenda"/"tender" is not.

**Banned words**
`revolutionary` · `game-changing` · `cutting-edge` · `world-class` · `seamless` · `empower` · `unlock` · `disrupt` · `leverage` · `frictionless` · `ecosystem` (meaning our product) · `In today's competitive landscape` · `thousands trust us` · `learn more` · `the future of work` as an unearned claim

**Banned framings**
marketplace · gig marketplace · jobs board · freelancer platform · talent network · quest platform · "Uber for X" · "Mechanical Turk but crypto"

**House vocabulary**

| Concept | Say | Never say |
|---|---|---|
| Person creating work | **Poster** | client, employer, requester, buyer |
| Person completing work | **Earner** | worker, gig worker, taskee, seller |
| Unit of work | **Task** | gig, listing, bounty, quest |
| The product | **App / application** | marketplace, platform, network |
| Payment event | **Settlement**, "paid" | disbursement, payout processing, withdrawal request |
| The mechanic | **The loop** | funnel, journey, pipeline |

## The message hierarchy

If a reader takes away only one thing, it must be #1. If they take three, they must be these three, in this order.

1. **You are paid the moment the task is accepted.** *(the benefit)*
2. **Post or work from anywhere — geography is not a gate.** *(the reach)*
3. **It's an application, not a marketplace — the work and the money happen in one place.** *(the reason #1 is possible)*

Every headline, subject line, hook and first sentence in this campaign leads with #1. #3 is never the opener — it is the explanation someone asks for after #1 has made them curious.

## The manifesto — for the site's About page, the launch video, and the founder's launch post

> Nobody ever quit a microtasking app because the tasks were too hard.
>
> They quit at the payout screen.
>
> They quit at the fifty-dollar minimum they'd never reach. At the thirty-day hold. At the dropdown that listed a hundred and ninety countries and let eleven of them withdraw. At the gift card for a shop that doesn't exist where they live. At the frozen account with no one to email.
>
> The work was never the problem. The work got done, on time, on a phone, on a bus, between two other things. The problem is what happens in the gap between finishing and being paid — because that gap is where every platform in this category has quietly kept its worst behaviour.
>
> So Tenda doesn't have one.
>
> A task is posted onchain. It's assigned. It's done. It's accepted. It's paid. Those are five states of one thing, in one app, and the last one arrives in the same moment as the fourth. Not a payout request. Not a withdrawal. Paid.
>
> That's also why Tenda isn't a marketplace. A marketplace introduces two people and steps back; the work and the money are somebody else's problem afterwards. Tenda is the place the work happens and the place the money moves — which is the only way "instant" can be true rather than advertised.
>
> Anyone can post a task from anywhere. Anyone can complete one from anywhere. The app never asks where you are before deciding whether you're allowed to be paid.
>
> Small work. Real money. No gap.
>
> **Tenda. Done and paid, in the same moment.**
